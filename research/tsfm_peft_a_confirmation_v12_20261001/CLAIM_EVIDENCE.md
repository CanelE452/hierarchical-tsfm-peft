# Claim–evidence boundaries

1. **Supported on the exposed development set:** adding the fixed learned Q to the same A main forecast improves pooled MSE/MAE relative to no Q and last-residual persistence. Source: q_contribution02.json plus q_uncertainty01.json; saved selected checkpoints, observed masks and paired time blocks. This is a same-checkpoint output comparison, not independently trained no-Q superiority.

2. **Supported conditional mathematics:** shared bias-free linear G preserves the exact Q space; complete uniform MSE then has a φ-independent full/main loss offset. With actual mask weights the difference is 2 J_bᵀ Wq. Source: actual model/loss code, old-data TRAIN/VAL contract scan, FP64 counterexample and 24 real paired backwards. Do not turn a few matching batches into full-trajectory identity or treat these as new Peacock backward tests.

3. **Not observed in the tested actual offset replay:** scheduler divergence on Robin/Hog. Their eight conditional replays kept the same LR schedules; Jena's VAL missingness prevents a constant-shift claim. The relative scheduler counterexample is synthetic. Source: equivalence_scheduler_replay01.json and installed PyTorch source receipt.

4. **Not established:** accuracy-gap resolution against full MSE-LoRA, independently optimized no-Q advantage, universal cost superiority, or cross-domain generalization. All three old-data full MSE-LoRA pooled MSE values remain lower. The prior native-LoRA MAE counterexamples stay in the main table. Peacock also retains external counterexamples: A's pooled MSE/MAE is 0.203266/0.315004, versus full MSE-LoRA 0.198176/0.298839, native LoRA 0.200123/0.299731 and F0 0.201521/0.300358. A is better than Direct NLinear's 0.219884/0.329450. The pooled MSE difference versus full MSE-LoRA is +0.005090 (+2.57%), conditional 95% interval [0.000155, 0.015748]; native-LoRA and F0 pooled MSE intervals include zero. All three alternatives' pooled MAE intervals favor them over A. Source: [confirmation_evaluation01.json](confirmation_evaluation01.json), role_summary and comparisons; lower point estimates alone are not universal superiority.

5. **Completed campaign-specific deployment tradeoff, without a stable speed-superiority claim:** the Peacock report retains all 135 GPU and 12 Direct CPU rows, three blocks and ten measured passes per configuration. At batch4, A full execution records 208.812 MiB peak allocated GPU memory and 153.39 origins/s. Full MSE-LoRA records 249.581 MiB/150.14 origins/s in full execution, 211.496 MiB/40.64 with chunks of 16, and 197.360 MiB/13.10 with chunks of 4. Thus tighter full-channel chunking can use less memory; A also has 2.568% higher pooled MSE and 5.409% higher MAE. The small full-execution throughput gap is within block variation and below the largest observed batch4 sentinel drift (pre/post latency −1.66% to +3.96%); it does not establish a stable speed advantage. Because A selects step0=LEVEL for both seeds, this tradeoff is not evidence of an internal-LoRA adaptation benefit. Four-thread Direct CPU batch4 records 16,456.30 origins/s at MSE/MAE 0.219884/0.329450 and remains a separate practical alternative; its GPU-memory fields are unavailable rather than zero. Fresh-group RAW and independently trained no-Q comparisons were not run. Source: [report_confirmation_values.json](report_confirmation_values.json), retaining all configurations and sentinels. Historical v11 tradeoffs remain separately labeled in cost_reference.json; no ratio combines campaigns.

6. **Prospective contract now selected under later delegation:** the original specified contract was not recovered; that historical gap is not rewritten as successful plan recovery. Subsequent explicit authorization delegated new-unit selection before the new fits and TEST evaluation. The resulting [confirmation_protocol.json](confirmation_protocol.json) fixes Peacock Education, C=13/K=4, with 40 TEST_A and 40 TEST_B origins. Saved selection and evaluation are complete for this one new building/use-type group in the existing electricity domain. The bounded exposure check found no earlier forecast-performance use of its building IDs; it does not prove complete historical non-use or absence from backbone pretraining. The completed cost observations in claim 5 do not extend these generalization or exposure claims. Sources: [selected_confirmation.json](selected_confirmation.json), [confirmation_evaluation01.json](confirmation_evaluation01.json), and the authorization supplement.

7. **Supported prospective E1 Q contribution at the selected checkpoint:** Peacock A_FULL improves over MAIN_ONLY and MAIN_LAST in MSE and MAE in each period and pooled. Pooled A-minus-MAIN_ONLY MSE is −0.095565 (−31.98%), conditional 95% interval [−0.105888, −0.086572]; A-minus-MAIN_LAST is −0.087226 (−30.03%), interval [−0.096148, −0.077954]. The saved intervals use 2,000 paired draws, seven-origin blocks within each period, and exclude parent/model/LR selection uncertainty. Period error sums and observed counts are pooled per channel, then macro-averaged, then averaged across seed losses; predictions are not ensembled. Source: [confirmation_evaluation01.json](confirmation_evaluation01.json). No independently optimized no-Q comparator was fitted, so this supports the E1 same-checkpoint contribution only.

8. **No selected latent-adaptation gain on Peacock:** both A seeds select epoch 0 at LR 1e−4; mean best VAL MSE ties across the two candidate LRs. Their selected predictor is therefore the zero-LoRA-update LEVEL parent. Saved A/LEVEL MSE and MAE are identical in each period and pooled, and both saved parent-Q parity checks have maximum absolute error zero. This preserves the Q result in claim 7 while withholding a claim that latent TSFM adaptation helped this unit. Sources: [selected_confirmation.json](selected_confirmation.json) and [confirmation_evaluation01.json](confirmation_evaluation01.json). Whole-trajectory no-Q equivalence remains untested.

Claims 1–3 describe the exposed-development explanation; claims 5–8 add a bounded prospective result with its negative adaptation finding, external accuracy counterexamples and measured deployment tradeoffs. They do not establish the broader superiority and generalization claims withheld in 4. Final implementation-check outcomes are reported separately in [finalconfirmationchecks.json](finalconfirmationchecks.json). Checks against saved values and agent review verify particular code/numbers; neither is independent scientific replication.
