# Fixed A: Q contribution and the limits of a no-Q equivalence claim

The method is unchanged from v11. For the fixed TRAIN PCA basis U, P=UUᵀ and Q=I−P, the selected latent LoRA predicts bφ(X)=Fφ(XU)Uᵀ. The frozen, previously trained LEVEL branch is q*(X)=repeat(last(XQ))+G*(XQ−repeat(last(XQ))). G* is the shared, bias-free linear temporal composition 512→32→48. The full predictor is bφ+q*. The completed old-data scope reuses v11 checkpoints without new fitting. A later, explicitly delegated prospective confirmation fits this same method and the prescribed comparators on the new Peacock Education group; it does not change A's formula.

The new output controls hold bφ fixed. MAIN_ONLY returns bφ, MAIN_LAST adds the last observed context residual, and A_FULL adds the full learned q*. FULL−MAIN measures the entire Q contribution; FULL−LAST isolates the additional learned temporal correction above residual-level persistence. MAIN_ONLY is constrained to P, so its large loss is not evidence against every independently trained compressed forecaster. The actual parent Q was reconstructed without loading the TSFM and agreed with the saved paired full/main outputs at the fixed tolerance.

On exposed Robin/Jena/Hog development periods, A_FULL reduced MSE and MAE relative to MAIN_LAST as well as MAIN_ONLY. The full-minus-last pooled MSE differences were −0.073841, −0.031360 and −0.465357. Conditional block intervals exclude zero for these pooled contrasts. The intervals condition on the already selected models and periods; they do not include repeated model selection or establish new-population generalization. Per-period, per-seed and channel results remain in the machine-readable outputs. External counterexamples are retained: uncompressed MSE-LoRA has lower pooled MSE on all three tasks, and native LoRA has substantially lower MAE.

For exact orthogonal P/Q and a shared linear temporal G, q* remains in Q. Under fully observed, uniformly weighted squared loss, the full and main-only losses differ by a φ-independent term. Under the actual masked channel-macro objective, however, their gradient difference is 2 J_bᵀ W q*, with w_tc=m_tc/(|V| n_c). A sufficient structural condition is P̄WQ̄=0; ordinary Euclidean orthogonality alone is insufficient. The 24 bounded old-data backward pairs exhibited very small FP32 differences on complete batches and nonzero differences beyond the fixed diagnostic tolerance on missing-target batches. Every audited old-data TRAIN contract contains at least some missing target occurrences. These diagnostics are not a new Peacock training-trajectory equivalence test.

An identical instantaneous gradient also does not guarantee identical training policy. Relative-threshold ReduceLROnPlateau is not invariant to arbitrary additive loss offsets. The scalar replay matched all twelve actual v11 A learning-rate histories. On Robin/Hog, the actual fixed-Q/VAL conditional offset did not change the replayed schedules; Jena's missing VAL targets precluded that constant-offset interpretation. A synthetic counterexample demonstrates the possibility, not an observed scheduler divergence in these actual runs.

The old-data no-Q refit is therefore omitted under the authorized same-checkpoint-claim branch, not declared mathematically redundant. Independently optimized no-Q superiority and whole-trajectory equivalence remain untested. This distinction is explicit in NO_Q_RUN_DECISION.json and must survive manuscript use.

P/Q error energies use a common complete-target-vector subset. They are not additive channelwise errors or an additive MAE decomposition. Hog's subset covers about 84.17% of target vectors and reverses A/MSE-LoRA's ordering relative to the primary masked metric. It cannot replace the primary evaluation or identify a unique cause of the remaining error.

The earlier missing-contract limitation remains part of the history: the located PLAN-named file was a request, not a recovered actual plan. Subsequent explicit prospective delegation authorized selecting and freezing a new contract before its confirmation fits and TEST evaluation. This produced Peacock Education, C=13 and K=4, a new building/use-type group within the existing building-electricity domain. It does not retroactively recover the old plan or establish a new domain. The bounded local exposure check found no prior forecast-performance use of these building IDs; complete historical non-use and absence of backbone-pretraining overlap are not established. The saved evaluation contains TEST_A and TEST_B, 40 origins each, with 11 model instances and 15 scored roles. Primary scores pool channel error sums and observed counts across periods, average across channels, and then average individual seed losses; they are not prediction-ensemble scores.

On this prospective group, the selected A has pooled MSE 0.203266 and MAE 0.315004. Holding its main output fixed, MAIN_ONLY has MSE 0.298832 / MAE 0.421165, and MAIN_LAST has MSE 0.290492 / MAE 0.395623. The saved A-minus-MAIN_ONLY pooled MSE difference is −0.095565 (−31.98%); A-minus-MAIN_LAST is −0.087226 (−30.03%). Their conditional 95% block intervals are [−0.105888, −0.086572] and [−0.096148, −0.077954]. MSE and MAE improve against both same-checkpoint references in each period as well. These intervals use 2,000 paired block draws of seven origins within each period and condition on the selected seeds; parent, model and LR selection uncertainty is excluded.

Both selected A seeds retain epoch 0 at LR 1e−4, with the two candidate LRs tied on mean best VAL MSE. Thus selection retains the zero-LoRA-update LEVEL parent predictor. A and LEVEL have identical saved MSE and MAE in both periods and pooled, and the saved Q parity checks have maximum absolute error zero for both seeds. The Q contribution is supported at this selected predictor; an additional benefit from adapting the latent TSFM is not demonstrated on Peacock. Independently fitted no-Q models remain unrun, and the E1 same-checkpoint comparison does not establish their inferiority or training-policy equivalence.

A's selected pooled MSE and MAE are respectively 2.568% and 5.409% higher than full MSE-LoRA's; sufficiently small full-channel chunks use less peak memory than A. The small A/full-execution throughput difference is within the observed block variation and below the largest batch4 F0 sentinel drift: sentinel pre/post latency changes range from −1.66% to +3.96%. Both selected A seeds are step0 LEVEL predictors, so the observed deployment tradeoff cannot be credited to an additional internal-LoRA adaptation benefit.
