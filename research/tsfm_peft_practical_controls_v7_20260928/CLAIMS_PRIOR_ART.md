## Claim being tested

The method combines a frozen latent-channel TSFM with a level-preserving temporal forecast of the discarded reconstruction component. Its proposed contribution is the particular allocation of forecasting work between those spaces, supported or limited by matched and practical comparisons. PCA, encoder/decoder adaptation, factor-plus-residual modeling, last-value normalization and low-rank temporal maps are established principles. They are not independent novelty claims.

The central v7 question is whether the existing LEVEL design remains a useful choice after adding a direct standard NLinear predictor and uncompressed TSFM inference split into small batches of independent series. The design itself is fixed. Robin is exposed after v6, and Jena 2024 has recorded earlier use. Neither v7 comparison is an independent protected confirmation. An unfavorable practical comparison can weaken the deployment claim without erasing the earlier LEVEL-versus-OLD/RAW results.

## Verified sources, overlap and limits of reading

**NLinear, AAAI 2023.** The title is *Are Transformers Effective for Time Series Forecasting?*, by Ailing Zeng, Muxi Chen, Lei Zhang and Qiang Xu. The final AAAI PDF's method passage, complete author `models/NLinear.py`, relevant `Dataset_Custom` scaling/split code and training/selection path were read. The model subtracts the detached last input, applies a temporal affine layer, and restores that value. Shared weights are the default (`individual=False`); per-channel layers are optional. `nn.Linear` default initialization is used: the alternative uniform-weight line is commented out. The official loader also fits `StandardScaler` on TRAIN, so external TRAIN standardization is not a difference unique to LEVEL. The official runner uses Adam and ordinary MSE and computes TEST loss during epochs while selecting by VAL. v7 preserves the shared affine model but follows its own masked objective, sampling and sealed-selection contract; it is not a complete reproduction of the original benchmark recipe. [Final paper](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26317/26089), [model](https://github.com/cure-lab/LTSF-Linear/blob/main/models/NLinear.py), [loader](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cure-lab/LTSF-Linear/main/data_provider/data_loader.py), [training code](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cure-lab/LTSF-Linear/main/exp/exp_main.py), [default options](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/cure-lab/LTSF-Linear/main/run_longExp.py).

**AdaPTS, ICML 2025.** The official PMLR entry verifies *AdaPTS: Adapting Univariate Foundation Models to Probabilistic Multivariate Time Series Forecasting*, its authors and publication. The accessible February arXiv v1 method sections were read in v6. In the v7 bounded review, the actual author implementation was read: `LinearAutoEncoder` and its transform/inverse-transform methods in `adapters.py`, plus `adapter_supervised_fine_tuning.make_predictions` in `adapts.py`. That path is transform → latent FM → inverse-transform; reconstruction loss is an optional separate objective. No observed-reconstruction-residual future correction is returned by that reviewed path. This code-level statement is narrower than asserting absence from every version or all related work. Latent adapters, dimension reduction and forecast-loss E/D adaptation overlap directly. COMPRESS is a structural main-path reference, not the full probabilistic AdaPTS method or its best tuning. The final proceedings PDF could not be read through the attempted official PDF/OpenReview endpoints; publication metadata, accessible preprint and current code are distinct evidence. [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/benechehab25a.html), [preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10235v1), [adapter implementation](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/abenechehab/AdaPTS/main/src/adapts/adapters.py), [prediction and optimization](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/abenechehab/AdaPTS/main/src/adapts/adapts.py).

**RevIN, ICLR 2022.** The verified title is *Reversible Instance Normalization for Accurate Time-Series Forecasting against Distribution Shift*. The author project and complete `RevIN.py` were read. The code removes and restores instance mean and scale and optionally learns per-channel affine parameters. LEVEL restores the last reconstruction-residual value without that variance normalization or affine transform. This is a difference in operation, not a first claim for reversible normalization. OpenReview's full paper was blocked by browser verification, and is not described as read. [Author project](https://seharanul17.github.io/RevIN/), [implementation](https://github.com/ts-kim/RevIN/blob/master/RevIN.py).

**Deep Factors, ICML 2019.** The official title is *Deep Factors for Forecasting*, by Yuyang Wang, Alex Smola, Danielle Maddix, Jan Gasthaus, Dean Foster and Tim Januschowski. The final paper Sections 3.1–3.2 and equations (3)–(7) were read. It combines global deep factors with local random effects and learns a probabilistic model. The general factor-plus-local-component construction predates LEVEL. Here the factors come from a frozen pretrained forecaster applied to fixed PCA series, and the complementary predictor is a shared deterministic linear map. The spatial constraint, fitting target and available information differ; this does not make the broader hybrid principle new. Its entire benchmark or GluonTS implementation was not reproduced. [Official record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/wang19k.html), [final paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/wang19k/wang19k.pdf).

## Conditional structural claims

Let X have shape L by C, let U have orthonormal columns, and put P=UU^T and Q=I-P. Let P_n(X) repeat X's final row n times. For a channel-shared bias-free temporal linear G, define N_G(X)=P_H(X)+G(X-P_L(X)). The fixed method is

\[
\widehat Y=b+\mathcal N_G(XQ)=b+\mathcal N_G(X)Q,
\qquad b=F_0(XU)U^\top.
\]

The equality follows from linearity and commutation of a shared temporal map with a channel projection. Therefore the method is explicitly a projected low-rank NLinear-style path beside the latent TSFM. Standard affine NLinear includes a bias and is not covered by that exact commutation without an additional condition on the bias. None of this algebra establishes useful forecasting performance.

Under the stated assumptions, the correction is annihilated by P, so the retained-space forecast remains b. A constant channel offset a with aP=0 is preserved through the discarded path. If G has temporal matrix A of rank at most 32, the equivalent level-preserving matrix is A_eff=A+(1_H-A1_L)e_L^T, with A_eff 1_L=1_H and rank at most 33. Equal trainable counts do not establish equal function spaces. Detailed proofs and missing-data limitations belong in APPENDIX; the projected interpretation and its limitations remain in the main text.

## Completed v7 Robin accuracy control

[robin_eval01.json](robin_eval01.json) reports direct NLinear combined MSE/MAE 0.327724/0.353410 under the selected learning rate 0.001. LEVEL remains 0.275196/0.324593, yielding 16.03% lower MSE; the fixed-seed paired-block interval for relative MSE difference is [−19.84%, −11.84%]. F0/LoRA's remaining accuracy advantage and the exposed follow-up status remain part of the main claim.

## Completed practical and diagnostic countercomparisons

All 117 GPU and 12 CPU rows completed with a hash-bound parity PASS. B4 M20 uncompressed F0/LoRA allocate 222,989,312 bytes, below original LEVEL's 224,908,288, while retaining the original uncompressed predictions and lower MSE. They process 74.73/64.39 origins/s versus original LEVEL's 254.29 center; thus allocation matching comes with a large measured throughput cost. Their reserved memory remains higher than LEVEL's. A memory-only claim of compression necessity is rejected, while the conditional three-axis tradeoff remains visible. Original/full-wrapper distinctions, block reversals and sentinel drift rule out an environment-independent general throughput claim. Direct NLinear is a much smaller and faster deployment alternative with the accuracy loss already quantified. [Cost records](cost_summary.json), [parity](microbatch_parity.json).

The corrected diagnosis computes per-seed errors before averaging and uses common complete target vectors. It finds remaining LoRA-relative loss in both P and Q, with Q contributing more in TEST-A and P in TEST-B. Positive excess is concentrated across channels but spread over multiple origins. These observations localize the deficit without proving that a change in K, G or normalization would fix it. The discarded initial ensemble diagnosis is not evidence; its correction history remains public. [Corrected source](robin_diagnostics_corrected_v7.json), [correction record](diagnostic_correction.json).

The [additive Q-error analysis](robin_q_residual_error_diagnostic_v7.json) distinguishes residual-correction errors from the corrected first analysis's total forecast errors. All-channel scatter atlases and the per-seed-first correlation table preserve both periods. They provide descriptive checks of the proposed context/error relation, not causal confirmation or new model selection.

## Completed Jena counterexample and retained contribution

The twelve Jena fits completed with no failures or reserve extensions. Under the fixed TRAIN/VAL recipe, LEVEL combined MSE is 0.324359 versus 0.323305 for OLD, 0.291159 for matched RAW, 0.273984 for direct NLinear, 0.292300 for F0 and 0.240590 for LoRA. LEVEL's 11.40% loss against RAW and 18.39% loss against NLinear have unfavorable combined directions in both seeds. TEST-B intervals for these comparisons include zero, and the periods are not treated as independent datasets. LEVEL remains better than ONLY and COMPRESS, so a useful learned correction and a useful residual-input restriction are different claims. [Actual evaluation](jena_eval01.json), [training](training_summary.csv), [all scores](accuracy_comparison.csv).

The defensible retained contribution is a limited design-and-control result in the tested building-electricity setting: LEVEL improves the earlier residual configuration and the specified direct NLinear control on Robin, with a conditional three-axis practical tradeoff. The fixed Jena result weakens a domain-general method claim and stays in the main manuscript. It does not establish that every electricity task benefits or every weather task fails. Numeric-degree wind direction, related derived weather variables and TRAIN-standardized channel-macro scoring limit interpretation; they are not demonstrated causes of the reversal. This study adds neither a new method nor a favorable replacement dataset after seeing that result.
